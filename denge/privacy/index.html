Privacy Policy

The short version. Denge looks at your photos and documents on your iPhone, and nothing it looks at is ever uploaded, to us or anyone else. There is no account and no advertising, and heyisoft runs no servers that receive your data.

Three kinds of data leave the device: an approximate location (never a photo, never its exact position) sent to Apple to look up city names, which you can switch off, purchase information with an anonymous identifier, used to check whether Denge Pro is active, and anonymous usage statistics and crash reports sent to Google Firebase: which screens and features are used and where the app crashed, never your photos, documents or anything you type. You can turn this off in Settings. There is no tracking and no advertising.

1. Who we are

Denge ("the app") is developed and published by heyisoft ("we", "us"). For any privacy matter, write to heyisoft@gmail.com.

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers the Denge app for iPhone and this website. It explains what the app handles, where that happens, and the choices you have.

4. What we do not collect

  • No photos, videos or documents, and nothing derived from them. They never leave your iPhone.
  • No account, sign-up, name, email address or phone number.
  • Anonymous usage statistics and crash reports (see section 5a) are the only diagnostics, and they contain no photos, documents, file or album names, labels, search text or locations.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers (the app never asks for one), no tracking across other companies' apps and websites, and nothing is ever sold.
  • No biometric data. See Face ID below.

5. Place names, and how to switch them off

To show which city a photo was taken in, the app rounds the location to the centre of a grid cell roughly 5 km across and sends only that point to Apple's reverse-geocoding service, which returns a place name. The exact position of a photo, and the photo itself, are never sent. Apple handles this request under Apple's privacy policy.

You can switch this off in the app's Settings with the Look Up Place Names switch under Places. From then on, nothing is sent.

5a. Usage statistics and crash reports (Firebase)

The App Store version of Denge uses Firebase Analytics, a service from Google, to count how the app is used. Google acts as a processor on our behalf. The statistics are not linked to your name, email address or any account, and they are not used for advertising or to track you across other apps and websites. Google Analytics data is kept for 2 months.

What is sent: which screens and features are opened, the rough size of your library and of a cleanup as a range, the steps of the paywall and purchase flow, the settings you change, standard information Firebase records such as device model, iOS version, app version and country, and a random identifier for this installation of the app. The app does not read your advertising identifier, and Firebase's vendor identifier collection is switched off.

Crash reports: if the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a technical report: where in the code the crash happened, device model, iOS and app version, free memory and disk space, and a random identifier for this installation (Firebase Installations). It contains no photos, documents or anything you typed. Crash reports are kept for 90 days.

Your choice: turn off Share Analytics in the app's Settings to stop both usage statistics and crash reports.

What is never sent: photos, videos, documents, file, album or category names, labels, search text, locations and place names, or any advertising identifier.

Google handles this data under Google's privacy policy and the Firebase privacy information. Deleting the app stops all of it. Debug and test builds send nothing.

6. Permissions and system features

  • Photos. Needed to read and organise your library. Deleting always goes through the iOS confirmation prompt, and deleted items go to Recently Deleted in the Photos app.
  • Files. The app only reads folders you explicitly choose in the Files picker.
  • Face ID. Optional, to lock the app. It is handled entirely by iOS. The app only learns whether unlocking succeeded, never any biometric data.
  • Notifications. Optional, for a weekly summary. It is scheduled on the device; nothing is sent through any server.
  • Background processing. Scanning can continue in the background, on the device.

7. Albums in Apple Photos

The albums Denge keeps up to date are created in your Photos library, in a folder called Denge. They stay in your library until you delete them, including if you delete the app.

8. Retention and deleting your data

  • The index and the "Deleted here" history stay on your device until you clear them in the app or delete the app.
  • Deleting the app removes its local data. Albums it created in Photos stay until you delete them.
  • Usage statistics are kept by Google Analytics for 2 months and then deleted.
  • Crash reports are kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days and then deleted.
  • Purchase records are held by Apple under Apple's terms, and subscription status by RevenueCat under RevenueCat's privacy policy.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export your personal data. We hold no personal data that identifies you: the app keeps your photos and documents on your device, and the usage statistics are anonymous. If you have a request or a question, write to heyisoft@gmail.com.
